# Research instructions

Read README.md and STATUS.md (when present) before continuing. Preserve previous results and source provenance.

## Objective

Find a legitimate direct cash mathematical prize and develop a correct, original, independently verifiable solution. Prefer finite certificates, short proofs, and modest computation. Check the original sponsor and current research, not only a catalog.

## Non-negotiable distinctions

Separate proved statements, reproducible computations, conjectures, heuristic expectations, and unverified claims. A finite search cannot prove a statement quantified over all integers. A modular zero does not prove an integer determinant is zero. A nonzero residue does prove the corresponding integer determinant is nonzero. An exact nonzero null vector proves singularity of its exact matrix.

Do not silently change a prize's sequence, indexing, alphabet, quantifiers, or required conclusion. Record the exact mathematical contract. Label the size and completeness of each search domain.

## Permissions

The user authorizes research and repository checkpoints. Do not contact sponsors, claim money, submit to journals, open external issues, or announce a claimed solution without explicit user approval. Preparing a local draft for review is permitted. Be accurate about AI involvement.

## Verification

Prefer mathematical arguments plus small, independent verification code. Use exact arithmetic or finite-field calculations with justified moduli. Reject or explicitly handle zero denominators. Document resource use, reproducible commands, and material limitations. Never count the same implementation run twice as independent verification.

## Files

Keep final research and code here. Keep binaries, downloaded third-party papers, caches, and temporary logs out of git. Cite sources by stable URLs and retrieval dates. Do not overwrite other research agents' files without coordination.
